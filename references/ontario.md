# Ontario Condominium — Jurisdiction Reference

Applies to corporations governed by Ontario's **Condominium Act, 1998** and its regulations.

---

## Reserve Fund Study Requirements

Under O. Reg. 48/01 (as amended):

| Study class | Description | Required frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Class 1 | Full study with site inspection | At registration; when no current study exists |
| Class 2 | Update with site inspection | At least every 3 years after a Class 1 or 2 |
| Class 3 | Update without site inspection | At least every 3 years (alternates with Class 2) |

**Practical cycle**: Class 1/2 → Class 3 (3 years later) → Class 2 (3 years after that) → repeat.

To check if an update is overdue:
- Add 3 years to the date of the most recent study
- If today's date exceeds that, the next study is overdue
- Overdue = flag 🔴 High (legal compliance risk)

---

## Form 15 — Notice of Future Funding

After each study, the board must issue **Form 15** to all unit owners, containing:
- Summary of the reserve fund study
- Summary of the proposed funding plan
- Statement of any differences between the study and the funding plan

Check whether Form 15 was issued after the most recent study.

---

## Reserve Fund Contribution Requirements

The corporation must collect contributions sufficient to ensure the reserve fund is
adequate for major repairs and replacements. The amount is set by the board based on
the reserve fund study. There is no statutory minimum percentage — the study recommendation
governs.

---

## Common Element vs. Exclusive Use vs. Unit

Under the Condominium Act and the corporation's declaration:
- **Common elements**: owned jointly by all unit owners; maintained by the corporation
- **Exclusive use common elements**: common elements designated for one owner's exclusive
  use (balconies, parking spots, lockers in some declarations)
- **Standard unit**: what the corporation maintains within a unit (defined by Standard Unit
  By-Law); anything above standard is the owner's responsibility

**Practical implication for financial analysis**: Equipment appearing in operating or
reserve expenditures should always be traced to whether it is common element or
owner-owned. Items that serve individual units exclusively (certain in-suite mechanical
components, interior finishes, balcony assemblies depending on the declaration) may be
owner responsibility. A corporation-run program for owner-owned components would typically
be an opt-in arrangement funded by participating owners, not a corporate capital expenditure.
Always verify against the declaration and standard unit by-law before characterizing any
expenditure as a corporate capital project.

---

## Status Certificate

A status certificate discloses the corporation's financial position to prospective buyers,
including known special assessments and material changes. Not a routine board document,
but may appear in the document corpus.

---

## Relevant Legislation

- **Condominium Act, 1998**, S.O. 1998, c. 19
- **O. Reg. 48/01**: General (reserve fund study requirements)
- **O. Reg. 49/01**: Description and Registration
- Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO): enforcement and dispute resolution body
  since November 2017

---

## Key Compliance Flags for Ontario Corporations

When analyzing financials for an Ontario corporation, check:

| Item | Flag if… |
|---|---|
| Reserve fund study | Most recent study > 3 years old |
| Form 15 | Not issued after most recent study |
| Reserve fund balance | Falling significantly below study projections |
| CAO annual return | May appear as a small expense line; absence may indicate non-compliance |
| Property management agreement | Management fee in line with disclosed agreement |
